Ten years ago an old runway jacket was something you found by accident. Now it has a market, a vocabulary and a price guide. Archive fashion has gone from collectors’ forums to the centre of the conversation, and resellers in New York, Tokyo and London are setting the terms.

The appeal is easy to understand. In a world of weekly drops, a piece that can’t be made again feels like the only thing with real scarcity. Owning it says you know the history, not just the hype.

> Nobody wants the new thing until someone tells them the old thing is gone.

But the boom has a cost. Prices have pushed younger fans out, fakes have followed the money, and some labels now reissue their own back catalogue as ‘archive’ to cash in on the mood.

What survives is the part that was always there: people who love clothes enough to study them. They were digging before it was a market, and they’ll keep digging when it isn’t.

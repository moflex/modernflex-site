For most of its history, rap was defined by where it came from. You could hear a city in a beat. That map is getting harder to read, and the artists breaking through this year seem happy about it.

Producers trade stems across continents in an afternoon. Rappers borrow cadences from scenes they’ve only visited online. The results can sound like nowhere in particular, or like several places at once.

> You used to need a plane ticket to hear a scene. Now you need a playlist.

The question is what gets lost. Local scenes still matter: they’re where artists learn in front of real crowds. The best new records know that, and carry a home town inside a global sound.

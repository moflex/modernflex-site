Streetwear is no longer just a uniform for skateboarding or hip-hop. It has become a shifting form of self-expression that borrows from every culture. Küki London takes its notes from Japan and works them into great capsule collections.

Küki London was founded in 2016 by Nico Constantinou and Eden Tarn, and launched its website, kukilondon.com, on 9 July that year. Since then the brand has run a number of pop-up shops, collaborated with Tower London and Granted, and supported artists and musicians including Kimmy Beatbox. Each collaboration is presented as a short film on the website.

Küki translates from Japanese as “air”, with a meaning close to “void of pure energy”. The brand’s mission is to “break the norm” and add something different to London streetwear with Japanese-inspired designs and gender-neutral collections.

Their latest capsule, Raidaball, is a range of baseball jerseys and caps mixed with Küki’s signature style.

As well as being a great clothing line, Küki London is a community that backs upcoming artists. The brand’s Instagram, with 23.6k followers, is full of fantastic street photography.

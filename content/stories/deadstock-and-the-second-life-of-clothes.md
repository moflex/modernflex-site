Kemi Adebayo-Clarke works out of a shared studio above a tyre shop. The rails are full of military surplus, unsold workwear and bolts of fabric rescued from a closing mill. Every piece she makes is a one-off, because the material runs out.

“I don’t think of it as sustainable fashion,” she says. “I think of it as fashion with a supply problem I’ve decided to love. When the cloth is gone, the design is finished.”

> When the cloth is gone, the design is finished. That’s the brief.

She sells through a mailing list and a single rail in a friend’s shop, and turns down most stockist requests. “The moment you promise forty of something, you’re buying new fabric. Then you’re just another brand.”

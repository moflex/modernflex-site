## Quick read

- **What happened:** Nvidia’s board accepted Jensen Huang’s resignation on 11 November after reports of two affairs, one of them reportedly with an executive US officials suspect of links to Chinese intelligence. On 23 November it voted unanimously to reinstate him.
- **The stock:** It went from a record $239.24 on 6 October to $125.53 last Friday, then rose about 28% pre-market on the news.
- **The history:** Jobs, Dorsey, Dell and Altman were all pushed out and came back. Across six famous CEO comebacks, the stock rose the next day five times, but four of the six trailed the market a year later.
- **The culture:** His black leather jacket, worn for 20+ years, went from signature look to meme template overnight.
- **The fun fact:** He was born on the same day as Michael Jordan. Same year, too.
- **The question:** Is a founder ever really replaceable?

## Twelve days

On Monday, Nvidia’s board did something it had said a week earlier it wouldn’t do. It gave Jensen Huang his job back.

He resigned on 11 November, after 33 years running the company he co-founded. CFO Colette Kress became interim CEO. Within days, more than 180,000 people had signed a “Stand with Jensen” petition, Elon Musk among them. On 17 November Huang posted that he’d made “serious mistakes” in his personal life and would be honoured to return. Hours later the board chair, Harvey Jones, told investors the board did “not intend to reinstate Jensen as CEO.”

Six days after that, it did. The board said it was satisfied with his candour and that input from senior US government officials left it confident his return posed no national security risk. The story isn’t closed: an independent committee review is ongoing, and Reuters reports SEC staff are looking at potential accounting issues. An inquiry isn’t a finding of wrongdoing.

## The comeback club

Founders being shown the door and walking back in is practically a Silicon Valley genre.

- **Steve Jobs, Apple:** forced out in 1985, back in 1997. The stock rose 78% in his first year back, while the S&P 500 rose 12%.
- **Michael Dell, Dell:** stepped aside in 2004, back in 2007. He took the company private in 2013 at $13.88 a share, 43% below the price when he returned.
- **Jack Dorsey, Twitter:** removed as CEO in 2008, back in 2015. The stock fell 61% in his first year back, while the S&P 500 fell 1%.
- **Sam Altman, OpenAI:** fired in November 2023, back five days later. There’s no public stock to measure.
- **Jensen Huang, Nvidia:** resigned on 11 November 2026, back on 23 November. The stock was up about 28% pre-market on day one.

The wider record, which also includes Howard Schultz at Starbucks and Bob Iger at Disney, shows markets cheer the return straight away and are much less sure a year later. Jobs is the exception everyone remembers.

The difference here is time. Jobs was gone twelve years. Huang was gone twelve *days*, while Nvidia reported $115.8 billion in quarterly revenue. The closest match isn’t Jobs. It’s Altman.

## How the jacket became a meme

The meme didn’t start with the scandal. Huang built it himself.

![One jacket, twenty years, four moments. Original illustration by MODERNFLEX.](/uploads/jensen-huang-jacket.svg)

- **2016:** In a Reddit AMA he called himself “the guy in the leather jacket who repeats things three times.” He says his wife and daughter picked the look.
- **The AI boom:** Fashion writers tracked his jackets to Tom Ford, including an $8,990 lizard-embossed biker. People tried to chart his jacket prices against Nvidia’s share price, and knockoff “Jensen” jackets went on sale for under $100.
- **2024:** He and Mark Zuckerberg swapped jackets on stage. Zuckerberg called him “like Taylor Swift, but for tech.” In Taiwan, the crowds that follow him have a name: “Jensanity.”
- **2026:** A viral appearance in Seoul, where he kept the leather on in sweltering heat, reportedly pushed searches for “leather jacket” up 130%. In July a Tom Ford jacket he’d worn sold at Sotheby’s for $960,000.
- **November:** Then the scandal hit, and the jacket became the internet’s shorthand for the whole story. There were AI-made memes about the man whose chips power AI, Coldplay kiss-cam edits, a leather jacket at half-mast outside Nvidia HQ, an #OpenToWork banner, and an ESPN host’s outfit dig on live TV. Some of the internet went the other way and crowned him an “S-tier CEO.”

That’s how it works for a founder who becomes a character. The recognition that makes you easy to mock is the same recognition that gets 180,000 people to sign a petition for you.

## Born on the same day as the GOAT

Here’s one the internet couldn’t leave alone. Jensen Huang was born on 17 February 1963 in Tainan, Taiwan. Michael Jordan was born on 17 February 1963 in Brooklyn. Same day, same year.

![Jensen Huang and Michael Jordan, both born 17.02.1963.](/uploads/jensen-huang-michael-jordan-birthday.svg)

Then, in the middle of the twelve days, Musk compared him to Jordan outright, calling him “Michael Jordan at his peak” and likening the ouster to the Bulls dropping Jordan mid-winning streak.

Does a shared birthday make Huang a GOAT? No. Astrology isn’t a valuation method. But the comeback parallel is hard to ignore. Jordan’s first return in 1995, announced with a two-word “I’m back,” brought three more titles. His second, with the Wizards, is the one people tend to forget. Comebacks can go either way, on the court and on the stock market.

We’re not settling the GOAT debate here. We’re just reporting what the timeline is saying.

## So, what makes a founder worth bringing back?

**Is the founder the company?** Nvidia’s numbers held up without him, yet the market priced his absence like a crisis.

**Does a persona protect you?** The jacket, the memes and the fans made Huang very hard to replace. Is that healthy, or does it make founders too big to hold to account?

**What does history say?** Comebacks get cheered on day one and are often behind the market a year later.

> Founders who reach pop-culture status end up as symbols.

Boards, markets and the internet all struggle to separate the symbol from the man in the jacket. The jacket’s back. The next twelve months will show whether this is a 1995 Jordan or a Wizards Jordan.

*Is a founder ever really replaceable? Let us know.*
